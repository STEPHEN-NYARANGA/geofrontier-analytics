Independent technical initiative

Making subseasonal forecasts more useful.

SeasonBlend is a statistical postprocessing system developed for the ECMWF AI Weather Quest. It offers a practical example of reproducible climate data engineering, forecast calibration and careful model evaluation.

What the system does

Two forecast windows. Three variables. A probabilistic answer.

The code takes a 101-member ECMWF extended-range ensemble, performs bias and probability calibration against historical ERA5 observations, and generates probabilities for five climatological quintiles.

The target variables are near-surface temperature, mean sea level pressure and weekly precipitation. Forecasts cover lead days 19 to 25 and 26 to 32 on the competition's global 1.5° grid.

The repository is a competition workflow. Its methods and outputs should not be represented as an independent public forecast warning service.

Forecasting workflow

Five connected analytical steps

Aggregate the ensemble

Calculate weekly means or totals from the ECMWF ensemble for the two target periods, keeping units, spatial coordinates and dates consistent.

Correct seasonal bias

Estimate historical errors against ERA5 using seasonally weighted observations, with spatial smoothing and a separate treatment of precipitation.

Calibrate probabilities

Adjust spread, count ensemble members across quintile thresholds and handle tied boundaries relevant to dry conditions.

Combine complementary evidence

Blend calibrated ensemble probabilities with recent observed frequencies and a climatological component, using fitted weights in three latitude regions.

Validate and export

Use historical folds, probability consistency checks and official package validation to produce the required NetCDF submission files.

Verification

A pipeline designed to fail visibly.

The repository documents automated tests, cross-check reports and validation of all six probability files. Its 19 November dry-run record reports 39 passing end-to-end checks, official package format checks and probabilities summing to one.

These are the project's documented results. This website has not separately executed the full ECMWF workflow or assessed its actual leaderboard position.

Open the detailed verification report

Checks reported in the workflow

  • Correct target weeks, dates and grid dimensions
  • Five finite quintile probabilities at each grid point
  • Probability sum of one within numerical precision
  • Agreement with the official submission package's format checks
  • Independent comparison with raw ensemble and recent observations
  • Separate experiment branches to protect the submitted model

What the experiments taught us

Negative results are useful results.

Competition-focused investigations are carried out separately from the submitted SeasonBlend model.

Calibration research

R1: more flexibility did not improve the baseline

Continuous latitude weights, additional bands, MJO-dependent weights and standard quantile mapping did not reliably outperform SeasonBlend in the documented 2026 tests. The project retained its simpler baseline.

Climate regime research

R2: a promising ENSO-informed temperature prior

An ENSO-conditioned prior improved temperature RPSS by approximately 0.0048 in a 2026 test with refitted weights, equivalent to roughly 0.0016 on the six-pair headline average. This remains an experimental candidate, not a change to the operational entry.

Interpretation and boundaries

What the evidence does not establish

The historical assessment is concentrated in 2026 and may not represent other ENSO states or multiple climate years. Reliability varies by variable and quintile. The November verification report also flags unusually sharp probabilities over parts of the tropical Pacific for the first pressure window and visible latitude-band boundaries in some maps.

Current results should therefore be interpreted as a competition-focused technical workflow, with continuing tests needed before claims of dependable regional or real-world forecast improvement. A successful file check is not the same as proof of scientific forecast skill.
