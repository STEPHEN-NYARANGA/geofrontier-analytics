Geography and environment

Earth observation and GIS

Satellite data can help show where landscapes are changing, how land is being used and where new patterns deserve closer attention. We combine image analysis with geospatial data management and mapping.

  • Land-use and land-cover analysis
  • Satellite image processing and change detection
  • GIS datasets, mapping and spatial analysis
  • Technical documentation and reproducible workflows

Typical question

Where has the landscape changed, and which areas need further investigation?

Methods considered

  • Sentinel and Landsat image preparation
  • Land-cover and time-series classification
  • GIS overlay, database preparation and map production

Possible deliverables

  • Study-area maps and georeferenced layers
  • Change summaries and reproducible workflows
  • Data-source and accuracy notes

Technical consideration: Clouds, seasonal timing, sensor resolution and limited reference data can affect how change is interpreted.

Infrastructure and energy

Ground motion and radar interferometry

Interferometric synthetic aperture radar can reveal subtle land-surface movement across time. We evaluate suitable satellite radar methods for exploratory ground-motion analysis, with attention to data quality and limitations.

  • Multi-temporal InSAR analysis
  • Ground deformation mapping and interpretation
  • Remote-sensing quality checks and uncertainty reporting
  • Support for research and exploratory site assessments

Typical question

Where is ground motion detectable, and what does the evidence support?

Methods considered

  • Multi-date SAR interferometry and time-series screening
  • Coherence, reference-point and atmospheric checks
  • Contextual comparison with terrain and other evidence

Possible deliverables

  • Exploratory velocity or displacement maps, where supportable
  • Time-series plots and quality-control records
  • Interpretation notes and recommended next measurements

Technical consideration: Satellite line-of-sight motion is not automatically a full three-dimensional displacement estimate. Low coherence and viewing geometry can limit conclusions.

Climate and sustainability

Climate and environmental analytics

From historical climate observations to environmental indicators, we help organise and analyse information that can support resilience planning and applied research.

  • Climate-data processing and visualisation
  • Environmental and spatial risk screening
  • Reproducible analyses and technical reports
  • Research support and stakeholder communication

Typical question

What do observations say about environmental pressures and exposure?

Methods considered

  • Processing historical and gridded climate datasets
  • Spatial indicator mapping and exploratory trend analysis
  • Interpretation of uncertainty and data representativeness

Possible deliverables

  • Analytical datasets, figures and spatial summaries
  • Technical reports with assumptions and caveats
  • Planning-oriented evidence briefs

Technical consideration: A hazard map is not a fully validated risk assessment unless appropriate exposure, vulnerability and reference evidence are available.

Digital tools and data science

Geospatial data engineering and AI

Some questions need a clear map. Others benefit from a reliable analytical workflow or a machine-learning model. We choose tools according to their value and validate results before drawing conclusions.

  • Python-based geospatial pipelines
  • Applied modelling and machine learning
  • Interactive maps and lightweight decision tools
  • Data quality, visualisation and technical training

Typical question

How can a recurring spatial analysis become dependable and easier to use?

Methods considered

  • Python and GIS workflow design with version control
  • Model evaluation and transparent performance reporting
  • Web mapping and dashboard prototypes

Possible deliverables

  • Documented analysis notebooks or pipelines
  • Interactive map or dashboard prototypes
  • Data dictionaries, QA checks and handover notes

Technical consideration: AI is used only where justified by sufficient data, suitable validation and a real benefit over a simpler method.

Climate and hazard intelligence

Forecasting and environmental monitoring

GeoFrontier intends to develop monitoring and forecast interpretation services for organisations facing climate and environmental variability. The scope includes flood observations, river forecast indicators, El Niño and Indian Ocean climate drivers, subseasonal outlooks, drought indicators and extreme heat analysis.

  • Flood mapping and interpretation of available river forecast products
  • ENSO and seasonal climate monitoring briefs
  • Subseasonal forecast calibration, verification and visualisation
  • Drought, heat and other environmental indicator tracking

Typical question

What has changed, what conditions might develop, and what does the scientific evidence support for the area of interest?

Methods considered

  • Satellite observations, reanalysis and suitable ground records
  • Climate indices, probabilistic ensembles and regional outlook interpretation
  • Historical verification, spatial exposure screening and uncertainty analysis

Possible deliverables

  • Flood and drought monitoring maps, dated and documented
  • Climate-driver and forecast-interpretation bulletins
  • Spatial dashboards, forecast skill summaries and technical reports

Technical consideration: Monitoring, risk screening and forecasting are not interchangeable. Outputs would be designed within the scope and evidence available, not offered as an unverified automatic alerting or official public warning system.
