How we work

Methods that can be explained. Results that can be examined.

Good geospatial consulting involves more than choosing a satellite product or algorithm. We focus on transparent decisions throughout the analysis.

The appropriate level of validation, documentation and delivery depends on the intended use and the evidence available.

A practical process

From the brief to a defensible deliverable.

A clear sequence with explicit checks and room for iteration.

Define

Understand the question and geographic context

Agree on the study area, intended decision, constraints, required outputs and the level of certainty the work must support.

Assess

Review the evidence before selecting methods

Examine existing datasets, sensor coverage, dates, spatial resolution, licensing, gaps and whether field observations are needed.

Analyse

Apply methods that match the data

Process remote-sensing and spatial datasets using appropriately documented workflows. Use advanced models when they add demonstrable value.

Validate

Check outputs and communicate limitations

Assess relevant accuracy, quality flags, sensitivity and uncertainty. Avoid conclusions that the available data cannot substantiate.

Deliver

Make the findings useful

Provide maps, geospatial layers, technical explanations or interfaces suited to the client's systems and audiences.

Scientific standards

Trust comes from the details.

These are working principles for project design, not claims of external accreditation.

Provenance

Record sources, acquisition dates, processing choices and the licence conditions for each dataset.

Reproducibility

Organise workflows so analyses can be reviewed, rerun or handed over when the project permits.

Uncertainty

Explain accuracy, reference-data limitations and where further ground information may change the answer.

Responsible data use

Consider confidential locations, sensitive infrastructure, privacy, data governance and the client's approval process.

What a project can include

Delivery should be as clear as the analysis.

Depending on scope and permissions, we aim to hand over materials that support review, communication and ongoing use.

Maps and data

Georeferenced maps and data layers with appropriate coordinate references, legends, units, scale information and metadata.

Technical explanation

Concise methods, source descriptions, assumptions, quality checks and interpretation in language suited to the client.

Reproducible workflows

Organised code, notebook or processing instructions when supported by the scope and intellectual property agreement.

Decision-ready communication

A brief summary of the main findings, what remains uncertain and what additional evidence would strengthen the conclusions.

Working relationships

Clear expectations from the beginning.

We intend to build engagements around a shared written scope, a feasible work plan and transparent communication of limitations.

Agree on the scope

Define the question, deliverables, data access, timelines, responsibilities and review points before work starts.

Protect information

Clarify rights to use datasets, privacy expectations, confidentiality and rules for releasing maps or results.

Review before release

Allow technical review and client feedback while preserving the distinction between evidence and interpretation.

Plan for handover

Agree on formats, documentation, maintenance needs and who will own or operate any resulting tools.
