GeoFrontier learning initiative

Start with curiosity. Learn to see the Earth differently.

We are developing educational programmes that bring satellite technology, maps, data science and responsible artificial intelligence into practical learning. The aim is not to teach everyone advanced coding. It is to help learners ask better questions about the places they know and gain the tools to investigate them.

Our educational purpose

From observing the world to understanding it.

A student looking at a river, a patch of forest or a rapidly changing neighbourhood is already asking a geographical question. Satellite imagery and GIS make it possible to explore those questions with evidence.

GeoFrontier aims to connect this curiosity with progressively deeper technical skills: visual interpretation, maps, geospatial data, Python, machine learning, deep learning, vision Transformers and carefully supervised agentic AI.

Our approach is grounded in scientific reasoning, inclusion and meaningful learning outcomes. We do not equate an AI demonstration with mastery of a subject.

Learning pathways

Different stages. Appropriate levels of challenge.

Each proposed pathway can be adapted to an institution's curriculum, resources, accessibility needs and learners' experience.

Foundations

Primary school

Discover what satellites do, practise reading maps, recognise land and water, and observe environmental change using familiar examples.

Possible activities

  • Compare two satellite images of a local landscape
  • Draw a simple map of a river, park or school environment
  • Discuss how observing the Earth can help communities

Learning outcome: Explain what a satellite image represents and describe a visible pattern.

Exploration

Secondary school

Build geospatial literacy through coordinates, image interpretation, introductory GIS and, where suitable, simple Python and data exercises.

Possible activities

  • Explore seasonal changes in vegetation or water
  • Make a basic thematic map using approved open data
  • Discuss why satellite images need careful interpretation

Learning outcome: Present a map and explain the data and uncertainty behind it.

Applied technical skills

TVET institutions and colleges

Apply geospatial methods to practical challenges using QGIS, field-data quality checks, Python automation and introductory classification methods.

Possible activities

  • Prepare a watershed or land-cover map
  • Clean, document and validate location-based datasets
  • Develop a repeatable geospatial workflow for a local use case

Learning outcome: Produce a documented practical output and complete a quality checklist.

Scientific computing

Universities and advanced learners

Progress from remote sensing and Python into geospatial machine learning, deep learning for imagery, Transformers, foundation models and human-supervised geospatial AI agents.

Possible activities

  • Evaluate classical machine learning against a deep learning baseline
  • Explore image segmentation, transfer learning and vision Transformers
  • Prototype a constrained agent that helps inspect and document geospatial data

Learning outcome: Deliver reproducible analysis with appropriate testing, provenance and error interpretation.

Learning outdoors

Take the map into the field.

Positioning is a practical way to connect satellites, real places and scientific reasoning. Learners can progress from supervised phone-based GPS activities to systematic GNSS field recording and accuracy checks suited to their level.

How GNSS supports scientific mapping

Schools

Locate approved landmarks and compare collected points with a simple map under teacher supervision.

TVET and colleges

Record location metadata, explore coordinate systems and produce a quality-checked GIS field layer.

Universities

Compare receiver methods, evaluate positional uncertainty and integrate GNSS with satellite or environmental records.

A learning progression

The right foundations come before the most advanced models.

Specialist AI topics are introduced only when learners have sufficient programming, data and evaluation skills. The progression is illustrative rather than a fixed national curriculum.

Observe

Earth observation and maps

Satellites, sensors, coordinates, image interpretation, local geography and environmental questions.

Investigate

GIS and scientific data

Open datasets, spatial analysis, Python fundamentals, exploratory plots and source quality.

Model

Machine learning and deep learning

Classification, model comparison, independent validation and computer vision for satellite imagery.

Advance

Transformers and agentic AI

Attention mechanisms, pretrained image models, tool-using assistants and human review of automated outputs.

Communicate

Real-world problem solving

Explain the results, document limitations and create a practical map, report or interactive demonstration.

Learning through real questions

Projects that start in familiar places.

These are suggested classroom exercises, not completed GeoFrontier programmes or verified hazard products.

Land and vegetation

How has our town's green space changed?

Students compare approved satellite images and produce a map with an explanation of scale and seasonal differences.

Water and environment

Where do we see seasonal water changes?

Explore open water observations while learning why cloud cover and image dates affect interpretation.

Climate and heat

Which surfaces appear warmer?

Compare vegetation and land-surface temperature, then discuss why surface temperature differs from air temperature.

Food and landscapes

What can satellites tell us about farmland?

Use vegetation and land-cover indicators while recognising that these are not direct measurements of crop yield.

Flood awareness

What does a historical flood map show?

Read a published event map, identify possible uncertainty and learn why emergency instructions must come from authorised agencies.

Advanced Earth observation AI

Can a model classify a satellite scene reliably?

Compare a simple method with a deep model, examine errors and test how results change on different locations.

Supporting educators

Equip teachers, not just devices.

We intend to co-design short modules, demonstrations and teaching materials with schools, colleges and universities. Educators are essential to the learning experience, especially for younger students.

Teacher development

Practical introductions to satellite imagery, open geospatial data, safe AI use and evidence-based classroom projects.

Accessible learning

Where internet connectivity is limited, use shared devices, teacher-projected imagery, printable maps and offline datasets.

Useful assessment

Focus on what learners can explain or produce, with simple reflection and feedback rather than inflated claims of proficiency.

Responsible learning

Children's safety and data rights come first.

Any programme involving minors must be planned with the host institution and its responsible safeguarding personnel before sessions, online activities or fieldwork begin.

Institution-led supervision

Schools or approved host institutions retain responsibility for student supervision, participation permissions and their own safeguarding procedures.

Privacy by design

Avoid collecting student identifiers, photographs, precise home or school-related personal location data unless there is a documented lawful need and appropriate permissions.

Age-appropriate technology

No unsupervised learner accounts or direct-to-child AI interactions. Advanced models and agentic systems are reserved for suitable groups with human oversight.

Honest qualifications

These are proposed workshops and capacity building programmes, not accredited qualifications. Formal recognition would require appropriate agreements and regulatory verification.

Partnerships and programme design

Build an experience that fits the learners.

GeoFrontier welcomes exploratory conversations with primary and secondary schools, TVET institutions, colleges, universities and education organisations about supervised, accessible satellite and AI learning.

Programmes will be designed for the host institution's context. No partner institutions, funded cohorts or programme launch dates are being represented as confirmed.

What we would establish together

  • Learner age, prior skills and learning outcomes
  • Teacher involvement, safeguarding and permission requirements
  • Available computers, connectivity and accessible learning materials
  • Suitable projects, data sources and teaching format
  • How learning progress and feedback will be assessed
