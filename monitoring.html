A connected view

Monitoring and forecasting answer different questions.

Monitoring describes conditions that have been observed or estimated, such as floodwater detected in a satellite scene, rainfall deficits or evolving sea surface temperatures.

Forecasting estimates possible future conditions. Forecasts become less certain as lead times grow and must be interpreted with appropriate skill evidence, probabilities and local context.

Our planned work links both with spatial exposure, infrastructure, terrain and stakeholder decisions, without treating model output as a verified impact prediction.

Hydrology and disaster risk

Flood monitoring and flood-risk information

Use earth observation, terrain and hydrological products to understand where flooding has occurred and where river conditions warrant closer assessment.

Work packages to develop

  • Satellite-observed flood extent from suitable radar or optical imagery
  • Assessment of historical flooding and changes in exposed land use
  • Interpretation of river flood forecast indicators from authorised providers
  • Spatial summaries for disaster planning, field verification and response partners
  • GPS and GNSS location capture for field checks and monitoring-site inventories

Possible outputs

Dated flood extent maps, catchment briefs, river forecast interpretation notes, GIS layers and decision support dashboards.

Limitation: River discharge forecasts do not directly show local inundation extent. GloFAS is primarily a river flood forecasting system and does not provide flash flood, coastal flood or real-time inundation forecasts. Satellite coverage and ground validation also constrain flood mapping.

Climate variability

El Niño, La Niña and climate-driver monitoring

Interpret the evolving El Niño Southern Oscillation (ENSO), the Indian Ocean Dipole and relevant tropical climate signals in the context of seasonal rainfall and temperature outlooks.

Work packages to develop

  • Periodic summaries of published ENSO and Indian Ocean Dipole indices
  • Explanation of probabilities from official seasonal outlooks
  • Regional teleconnection analysis with historical observations
  • Climate-driver briefings for planning and stakeholder communication

Possible outputs

Monthly climate-driver bulletins, anomaly charts, scenario briefs and sector-focused interpretation of official forecasts.

Limitation: An El Niño or La Niña phase is not a deterministic forecast of rainfall or flooding at a particular place. Local effects vary by season and region; any inference must be assessed against observed patterns and regional forecast guidance.

Weather and climate outlooks

Subseasonal and seasonal forecasting support

Develop statistical postprocessing, verification and interpretation of probabilistic temperature, precipitation and related forecast information for planning horizons beyond day-to-day weather.

Work packages to develop

  • Calibration and evaluation of suitably licensed forecast ensembles
  • Subseasonal summaries on weekly timescales
  • Seasonal anomalies, probabilities and forecast skill diagnostics
  • Hindcasts, reliability plots and stakeholder-ready forecast briefs

Possible outputs

Forecast verification reports, anomaly and probability maps, customised outlook briefs and reproducible workflows.

Limitation: Skill varies by variable, region, season and lead time. This page does not claim a validated GeoFrontier operational forecasting product. The founder's independent SeasonBlend competition work is separate from any company service.

Water and agricultural systems

Drought and rainfall-deficit monitoring

Combine precipitation, vegetation, evapotranspiration and soil moisture indicators to identify changes relevant to water availability, agriculture and ecosystem conditions.

Work packages to develop

  • Rainfall anomalies, dry spell and seasonal deficit analysis
  • Vegetation and soil moisture indicator mapping where data permit
  • Historical drought-event comparisons and spatial summaries
  • Interpretation for agriculture, water and environmental programmes

Possible outputs

Drought indicator maps, time-series dashboards, historical comparisons and data quality notes.

Limitation: Meteorological, agricultural and hydrological drought describe different processes. A single index cannot establish local crop failure or water scarcity without contextual evidence.

Thermal environments

Extreme heat and heat-stress assessment

Analyse temperature and humidity patterns to support environmental planning, exposure screening and the evaluation of heat-sensitive locations.

Work packages to develop

  • Historical temperature and heat anomaly assessments
  • Spatial urban heat and land surface temperature mapping
  • Screening of heat-stress indicators where suitable inputs exist
  • Interpretation of published heat outlooks for planning purposes

Possible outputs

Heat maps, trends, exposure-screening briefs and interpretable spatial summaries.

Limitation: Land surface temperature is not air temperature, and heat-stress indices require specific meteorological inputs. This is environmental analysis, not personal medical advice or a public heat-warning service.

Environmental observation

Land, water and infrastructure change monitoring

Track measurable landscape changes using satellite time series and GIS, helping organisations identify places for further investigation.

Work packages to develop

  • Land cover, vegetation and watershed change detection
  • Waterbody and wetland observations from suitable imagery
  • Exploratory ground-motion trends from radar interferometry
  • Mapping of monitoring priorities for field teams
  • GNSS-supported reference observations for field validation

Possible outputs

Time series, GIS layers, map atlases, change reports and exploratory dashboards.

Limitation: Satellite observations complement field data. Ground movement does not by itself predict a landslide, and change-detection signals may reflect sensor or seasonal effects.
