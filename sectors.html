Public institutions

Government and public services

GIS and Earth observation can help institutions understand land use, monitor environmental change and develop clearer evidence for local and regional planning.

  • Spatial data inventories and quality assessments
  • Land-cover monitoring and administrative mapping
  • Mapping support for public planning and reporting

Questions to explore

How is land use changing, and what spatial evidence can inform planning priorities?

Potential outputs

Administrative and thematic maps, GIS data inventories, change assessments and policy-ready visuals.

Important: Official boundaries, permissions and reference data must be checked with the relevant authority.

Built environment

Energy, mining and infrastructure

Satellite observations can complement field measurements when evaluating terrain, potential movement and changes around large sites and linear infrastructure.

  • Exploratory InSAR ground-motion analysis
  • Environmental and site-context mapping
  • Time-series analysis and evidence summaries

Questions to explore

Where might monitoring reveal changes relevant to land stability or asset management?

Potential outputs

Exploratory InSAR analyses, site-context maps, time-series figures and limitations notes.

Important: Satellite estimates complement but do not replace engineering surveys, site inspection or safety certification.

Natural systems

Environment and climate resilience

Well-documented spatial assessments can support environmental monitoring, land stewardship and early-stage risk screening.

  • Landscape and ecosystem change detection
  • Terrain, surface-water and climate-data analysis
  • Maps and reports for environmental decision-making

Questions to explore

What indicators can help describe environmental pressure over time?

Potential outputs

Land-cover or surface-water summaries, environmental indicators and technical evidence briefs.

Important: Risk conclusions require reliable context, exposure information and appropriate independent evidence.

Land and livelihoods

Agriculture and water resources

Satellite and geospatial datasets can reveal seasonal and longer-term changes across agricultural landscapes and river basins.

  • Land-use, cropping-area and vegetation analysis
  • Watershed and catchment mapping
  • Integration of remote sensing with local observations

Questions to explore

How are landscape conditions varying across seasons and locations?

Potential outputs

Vegetation and land-use maps, watershed data layers and monitoring summaries.

Important: Satellite proxies should not be treated as direct crop-yield or water-availability measurements without validation.

Knowledge partners

Research and development organisations

Support for teams that need reproducible geospatial analysis, careful documentation, independent checks or accessible visual explanations.

  • Scientific computing and research workflows
  • Technical maps and interactive visualisation
  • Training and method documentation

Questions to explore

What analysis, reproducibility or visualisation support is needed?

Potential outputs

Documented workflows, data preparation, methods review, visualisations and tailored training.

Important: Academic authorship, data access, confidentiality and intellectual property should be agreed explicitly.
